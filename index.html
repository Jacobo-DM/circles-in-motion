Circles in Motion

Circular movement, regulation, presence, adaptation, and return.

Circles in Motion is the umbrella. It holds movement practices that use arcs, spirals, rhythm, breath, balance, and return to help the body listen, regulate, express, and reconnect.

RageDance is one branch within Circles in Motion. It is not the whole. Other branches may be quieter, more playful, more adaptive, more restorative, or more communal.

This commons begins from a simple premise: movement was never only for performance. It can be a way of tending attention, meeting strong feeling, restoring mobility, and remembering relation.

Begin where you are. Move what can move. Let the circle teach return.

Branch map

The umbrella is larger than any one practice.

Circles in Motion can hold fire, play, adaptation, classroom use, restorative movement, martial clarity, accessibility, and story. Branches may grow at different speeds.

  • RageDance
    The originating fire branch: strong feeling, rhythmic discharge, breath, safety, ethics, and embodied return.
  • Baile de los Círculos
    A branch-name held for Spanish-language, communal, and culturally responsive forms of circular movement practice.
  • CIM on a Chair
    An emerging seated module: adaptive, accessible, and still capable of full cardio. A reminder that movement is not confined to standing, jumping, or performing.
  • Winter Sequence
    A possible cold-weather pathway for small spaces, limited warmth, low energy, and careful ignition.
  • Elephant Circle
    A grounding circle practice connected to classroom presence, group rhythm, and collective regulation.
  • Double Arc Standing
    A possible standing form for balance, bilateral awareness, and counter-turning arcs.
Shared principles

What belongs across the branches?

These principles are not rules of performance. They are safety, attention, and design anchors for any practice held under Circles in Motion.

01
Clarity before intensity

If the movement grows faster or stronger, attention must deepen. Speed is never a substitute for presence.

02
Adaptation is discovery

A chair, wall, cane, floor, doorway, or smaller range of motion does not make the practice lesser. It reveals another doorway.

03
The circle teaches return

Arcs, spirals, turns, pauses, and counter-turns help the body notice where it is, where it has been, and how it may come back.

04
No correct body

The practice must not become another system of shame. Begin with the body present, not the body imagined.

Movement was never only for performance. The body may also be a field of listening, shelter, warning, play, repair, and return.
Emerging modules

CIM on a Chair belongs here.

Chair-based Circles in Motion began as a practical response to cold, constraint, and the need to keep moving when full standing practice was not the right doorway. It can still be vigorous. It can still be full cardio. It can still be beautiful.

This module may become an accessibility bridge for injury, limited mobility, classroom settings, elder practice, small rooms, winter practice, and Regenerative Gaming story-work.

Its deeper teaching is simple: the wing was never only for flying.
